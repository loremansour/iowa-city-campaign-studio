Contact & project planning

A focused campaign starts with a useful brief.

Define what you want to promote, who you want to reach, and what action you want them to take. These questions outline how a project inquiry would begin.

This educational demonstration does not accept inquiries, bookings, or payments. No personal information is collected through this page.

What would go into your project brief?

A clear starting brief helps connect the business need with the campaign's scope, creative direction, and timeline. Use the six prompts below to organize a hypothetical project.

01 · Business goal

What are you introducing or promoting? Describe the service, event, or offering and the business outcome you want the campaign to support.

Example: Introduce a beginner fitness class and encourage introductory bookings.

02 · Intended audience

Who needs to see this campaign? Describe their location, interests, needs, and familiarity with your business. Identify any questions that could prevent action.

Example: Iowa City adults interested in strength training who have never attended a class.

03 · Timing

When does the campaign need to launch? Note any event dates, approval deadlines, production availability, and periods when the audience needs to receive reminders.

Example: Publish introductory content three weeks before the first class.

04 · Creative needs

What materials already exist, and what needs to be created? Consider photography, short-form video, graphics, messaging, and a campaign landing page.

Example: One class walkthrough video, instructor portraits, and a question-and-answer carousel.

05 · Budget and scope

Identify a proposed budget range and the work it needs to cover. Separate creative production, paid promotion, and any outside expenses so priorities are clear.

Example: Prioritize one filming session and reusable social assets within a defined project budget.

06 · Success measures

What action should someone take after seeing the campaign? Identify the relevant starting numbers and how the intended action would be tracked.

Example: Track visits to the class schedule and completed introductory bookings.

How the brief shapes a project.

The goal and audience guide the campaign message. The timeline and budget help define a realistic production scope. The intended action determines which content and measurement methods belong in the launch plan.

A proposed scope would identify deliverables, responsibilities, review stages, and a handoff date before production begins. Those decisions keep the project connected to its original goal.
